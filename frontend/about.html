About the project

Learning History by Talking to It

A graduation project that turns history lessons into conversations: students ask, and historical figures answer in their own voice.

Our Vision

Traditional history lessons ask students to read and memorise. We wanted to make learning about historical figures interactive and memorable.

Instead of searching through long texts, a student can ask Salah El-Din Al-Ayyubi or Muhammad Ali Pasha directly, follow up on what is unclear, and fill their own knowledge gaps.

Our Mission

To make historical learning more accessible, interactive and engaging, so that history sparks curiosity instead of feeling like a list of dates.

By combining retrieval-augmented AI with 3D avatars and Arabic speech, the project bridges the gap between past and present and turns passive reading into active dialogue.

What Students Gain

  • Real-time conversations that make lessons engaging
  • Answers to their own questions, not only the textbook's
  • Better retention through conversation and storytelling
  • Sources for every answer, to read further and check facts
  • Spoken Arabic practice through voice questions and answers

Project Impact

  • Makes historical education more engaging and interactive
  • Helps students remember through conversational learning
  • Gives each student a personalised learning experience
  • Bridges traditional textbooks and modern technology
  • Inspires a new generation to learn from history
Technology

How It Is Built

An open, reproducible stack. Everything runs on free services.

BackendPython, FastAPI, served with Uvicorn or Docker
Knowledge sourceArabic Wikipedia, downloaded through the MediaWiki API and split by section
Retrieval (RAG)SILMA Arabic sentence embeddings and a FAISS vector index, built with LangChain
AnswersFree Groq API (Qwen 3.8, GPT-OSS, ALLaM) or Google Gemini, prompted to answer in the first person, in character and fully vowelled
SpeechMicrosoft Edge neural Arabic voices with word timings (ElevenLabs optional); speech recognition in the browser
3D avatarTalkingHead (Three.js) with a custom Arabic lip-sync module that maps Arabic letters and vowels directly to mouth shapes

The characters are AI simulations built from encyclopedia text. They can make mistakes or simplify events, so treat answers as a starting point and check the linked sources.
